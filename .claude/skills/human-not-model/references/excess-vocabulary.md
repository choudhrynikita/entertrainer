# Excess vocabulary and GPT-isms

Empirical lists. Tier-1 is a hard fail. Tier-2 is a cluster warning. Constructions are counted separately because Kobak measured words, not syntax.

## How to use

- Scan title, dek, headings, body, captions, alt text.
- Inflections count (*delve / delves / delving / delved*).
- Literal sense may pass (*unlock a door*, *foster child*). Metaphor sense fails.
- Do not fail the product name **Elevate**.
- One Tier-2 word in 1,500 words is noise. Two in 400 words is a pack.

Evidence: Kobak et al. excess ratios, Juzek & Ward focal words, and editor inventories that travel with them.

## Constructions — count, then cap

| Pattern | Catalog | Gate |
|---|---|---|
| It’s not X. It’s Y. / This is not about X. It’s about Y. | 1–2 typical | **max 2** |
| A, B, and C triads | rare | max 3 per piece |
| Not just X — also Y | occasional | max 2 |
| From X to Y range | rare | max 1 |
| “Imagine…” opener | used, once | max 2 |
| “Here’s the thing” | almost never as filler | 0 as filler |

## Humanizer residue — also fail

Deliberate typos, “hte”, random comma splices added for “burstiness”  
Slang the catalog does not use: hits different, chef’s kiss, let that sink in, rizz, unalive, living rent-free  
“As a human, I…”  
Synonym cycling in one paragraph (use / utilize / employ / leverage)  
Em-dash as the default clause joiner in >12% of sentences

## Allowed in this house (do not flag)

Eg:  
Actually / Well… / Maybe / Imagine / Basically — **once or twice, earned**  
Centre, behaviour, analogue (Indian/British spelling)  
GST, WhatsApp, cricket, group chat, CQA, intern  
“Read that again.” / “Read that slowly.”  
“This was never really about X.”  
“Where I have to be honest”  
A zip, a fridge, a bus, a door, a balloon — concrete objects
